The Plain and Simple Truth

An exploration of propaganda from the Soviet Union

CONTEXT: The USSR sought to rapidly expand their agricultural production through collectivization, which meant all private farmland became state owned. Farmers were resistant to this change so the government used coercion and violence to gain ownership of the land. Agricultural production initially decreased which resulted in a widespread famine in 1932-1933. It's estimated that 5-8 million peasants died from starvation. Peasants who did not meet their quotas were punished with fines, confiscation of land and equipment, and in extreme cases deported or exiled. Peasants who were caught hiding food were considered enemies of the state and were executed. What was said to be a cooperative mobiliztion of peaseants to bring prosperity and empower the state actually brought scarcity, suffering, and lead to the deaths of many citizens. Learn more

CONTEXT: Following the Russian Revolution, the USSR Leader Joseph Stalin implemented a series of five-year plans to rapidly industrialize the country. The goal of industrialization was to expand Russia's production sectors like electricity, mining, manufacturing, and agriculture. Another reason was a rising fear of potential invasion from a more developed country. The government used propaganda to promote the benefits of industrialization and to encourage citizens to work hard and support the government's goals. The majority of the food produced by the newly collectivized farms was allocated to urban areas for industrial workers. Despite this poster presenting front-line work as exciting and voluntary, much of the labor force was made up of prisoners and forced laborers. While growth was achieved, citizens were not thriving as towns became overpopulated because of people migrating from rural areas. This poster tries to portray USSR industrialization as a positive and empowering movement, but the reality was that many citizens were suffering and dying from famine, forced labor, and harsh working conditions. CONTEXT: To meet the demands of rapid industrialization the USSR government campaigned to mobilize the labor force and encourage citizens to work hard and support the government's goals. Despite having some successes, quotas were generally unrealistic and contiued to be increased even when the initial quota was not met. To be a good citizen of the Motherland was to be a good worker. Although, many times workers were forced into labor camps called Gulags. Unemployment relief programs were also abolished and many citizens were sentenced to work in labor camps for only minor offenses. This poster shows labor as an honorable and patriotic duty, but much of the labor was demoralizing, involuntary, and life-threatening. Learn more

CONTEXT: While it is true that the USSR played a crucial role in defeating Nazi Germany during World War II, they intially signed a non-aggression pact with Germany in 1939, which allowed Hitler to invade Poland and start the war. The USSR also invaded Poland from the east, and occupied the Baltic states of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania. The USSR's actions during this time were motivated by their own strategic interests, rather than a desire to defend against fascism. They only joined the Allies after Germany invaded the Soviet Union in 1941. The USSR's role in defeating fascism should be acknowledged, but it is important to recognize that their actions were out of self-interest. They used propaganda to portray themselves as defenders of the people but their actions evidently resulted in the deaths of millions of their own citizens. Learn more

CONTEXT: I key part of the USSR authoritarian regime was the indoctrination of the children and youth. Children in the soviet union were treated well and provided education in the hopes of raising a generation of loyal communists. This indocterination is often to referred to as the "Cult of Childhood". A lot of propaganda depicted a happy childhood that was a direct result of the success of the state which helped to encourage loyalty to the government. This idealized version of childhood was not, however, the experience of all soviet children. Due to the famine and parents being arrested many children were orphaned and had to live on the streets or in orphanges that were in terrible conditions. The propganda of a happy childhood purposefully neglects to mention the suffering of many children in the soviet union. Learn more

CONTEXT: Joseph Stalin was the leader of the Soviet Union from 1924 until his death in 1953. Stalin used propaganda to portray himself as a benevolent and heroic leader who brought prosperity to their society and should be celebrated. Behind his cult of personality, however, his actions told a very different story. He gained control by eliminating political rivals and replacing them with loyalists and much of his campaigning centered around exposing so-called enemies of the people both domestic and foreign. He frequently used violence to maintain control and silenced independent voices by either killing or deporting them. Stalin was a brutal dictator that did not care about the well being of Soviet citizens and by the end of his life millions of lives had been lost. Learn more

Exhibition Statement

The purpose of this exhibition is to the explore the role propaganda played in the Soviet Union. Propaganda was used to create a grandiose narrative that would shape public opinion to help the USSR government maintain control. This site gives viewers crucial context for events that happened in the Soviet Union that were either purposefully left out of the propaganda or twisted to fit their government's intended narrative. Viewers are encouraged to dig deeper for important information and not just accept things at face value. Propaganda is a powerful tool that has been, and is still, used to control populations. Disinformation is more prevalent than ever because of the reach the internet has; so it is important that the general public maintains a healthy amount of skepticism when presented with new information. Especially when the information is coming from people in positions of power.
